How much does n8n automation cost for small business?

n8n is a workflow automation platform. Instead of paying someone to do repetitive tasks by hand, you build a workflow once and it runs on its own. For small business owners, the real cost question is not just the software fee, it is whether the time and money you save justify the setup work.

Nimish Rangani28 October 20266 min read

This post breaks down what you actually pay, what affects the price, and when automation is worth doing.

What you pay for n8n

n8n has a free tier and paid plans. The free tier lets you run workflows and connect to most tools at no cost, but it has limits on how many tasks your workflows can perform each month.

Paid plans charge based on how many workflow executions you use. An execution is one run of your workflow. If you automate invoice reminders and your workflow sends 100 invoices a month, that is 100 executions.

You can self-host n8n on your own server or use n8n Cloud (their hosted version). Self-hosting has no per-execution fee but costs you time and infrastructure. Most small businesses find the hosted version simpler.

When the costs add up

The n8n platform fee is usually the smallest part of the total cost. You also need to think about:

  • API costs from other tools. If your workflow sends emails, posts to Slack, or queries a database, those services may charge per action.
  • Time to build and maintain the workflow. Someone has to design it, test it, and fix it when things break.
  • Data storage. If your workflow collects or moves data, you may need a database or storage service.

For example, imagine a small accounting practice that automates invoice reminders. The n8n execution cost might be low, but if the workflow integrates with your email provider, accounting software, and CRM, you are paying three separate services. Each one has its own pricing. That is where the real expense sits.

Fixed costs versus variable costs

n8n's execution-based pricing is variable: you pay more when your workflows run more often. This is different from hiring someone, which is a fixed monthly salary.

Variable pricing is good if your workload changes. In a quiet month, you pay less. In a busy month, you pay more. But you need to know your monthly execution volume to estimate the bill.

Start by counting how many times the task runs per month. If you send 50 invoices a month and each invoice triggers one workflow execution, that is 50 executions. Check n8n's current pricing page to see what that costs on their plan level.

When automation makes sense for small business

Automation is worth the effort if the task is repetitive, runs often, and takes time to do by hand.

Good candidates: - Tasks you do the same way every time (sending reminders, logging data, moving files between tools). - Tasks that run multiple times a week or more. - Tasks that take more than a few minutes each time. - Processes that need to run outside business hours or overnight.

For example, if you send cold email follow-ups to prospects, automating the send and logging the replies saves hours every week. Or if you receive orders and need to create an invoice, send a confirmation email, and update your stock sheet, automating that workflow removes manual steps and reduces errors.

When automation is not worth it

Be honest about when not to automate.

Skip automation if: - The task runs rarely (once a month or less). Setup time will not pay off. - The task is too complex or unpredictable. If every case is different and needs human judgment, automation adds little value. - The task takes only a minute or two. The time saved does not justify the setup cost. - You are not sure which tools you need yet. Rushing into automation before your process is stable wastes money.

Also, if you do not have budget for the platform, APIs, or a database, and you cannot find time to build it yourself, wait. Automation is not urgent if your current process works and you are not drowning in manual work.

How to estimate your total cost

Work through these steps:

  1. Pick the task you want to automate. Write down exactly what it does.
  2. Count how many times it runs per month.
  3. List all the tools and services the workflow will connect to (email, CRM, accounting software, database, etc.).
  4. Check the pricing of each service. Look for execution fees, API call limits, or monthly charges.
  5. Estimate the time to build the workflow. If you are doing it yourself, count your hours. If you are hiring help, get a quote.
  6. Add up: platform cost + API costs + build time (in money) + any database or hosting fees.
  7. Compare that to how much time the automation saves you per month (in money, based on your hourly rate).

If the savings are higher than the costs within a few months, it is worth doing. If the payback takes a year or more, reconsider.

Reduce costs with a simple start

You do not have to automate everything at once. Start small.

Begin with the task that wastes the most time or causes the most errors. Build a simple workflow that does just that one thing. Once it works, you can add more tasks later.

Also, use free or low-cost tools where you can. Many services offer free tiers or free APIs for small volumes. PostgreSQL and Supabase are free to start with. Some email providers offer free plans. The less you pay for supporting services, the lower your total cost.

One more tip: test your workflow thoroughly before it goes live. A broken automation can cause bigger problems than doing the work by hand, so spend time on testing.

Common questions

Is n8n free?

n8n has a free tier that covers small workflows with limited monthly executions. Paid plans unlock higher execution limits and more features. Self-hosting is also free from an n8n perspective, but you pay for your own server or cloud infrastructure.

What is an execution in n8n?

An execution is one complete run of a workflow. If your workflow sends an email, logs data, and creates a record, that is one execution. If the workflow runs 100 times a month, that is 100 executions.

How long does it take to build a workflow?

It depends on complexity. A simple workflow (send an email when you receive a form) might take an hour. A complex one with multiple steps, error handling, and data lookups can take days. Start simple and add features as you go.
