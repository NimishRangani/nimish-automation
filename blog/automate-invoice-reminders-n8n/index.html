How to automate invoice reminders in n8n

Late invoices hurt cash flow. Most businesses send reminders by hand, which means they get forgotten or sent inconsistently.

Nimish Rangani26 October 20267 min read

Automating invoice reminders in n8n means unpaid invoices get flagged and customers get nudged at the right time, every time. You control the timing, the message, and the conditions that trigger each reminder.

Why automate invoice reminders

When invoices go unpaid, you chase them. When you chase them manually, some slip through. A reminder that goes out automatically on day 5, day 15, and day 30 after the due date means no invoice is forgotten.

Automation also removes emotion and inconsistency. Every customer gets the same treatment. You spend your time on customers who actually need a conversation, not on sending emails.

The other win is data. An automated system logs when reminders were sent, who opened them, and when payment arrived. That tells you which customers are reliably late and which reminders actually work.

What you need before you start

You need three things in place:

  1. A list of unpaid invoices with due dates. This usually lives in your accounting software (Xero, Wave, QuickBooks) or a spreadsheet. You need to be able to pull it out regularly.
  1. Customer email addresses linked to each invoice. Check that your accounting software exports this cleanly.
  1. An email account to send reminders from. This can be your company email or a dedicated reminders address. Make sure you have the SMTP details or can connect via an API.

You also need to decide: when should reminders go out? A common pattern is day 3 after due date (gentle first nudge), day 10 (second notice), and day 20 (final notice). You can adjust this based on your customers.

Building the workflow in n8n

Start by creating a new workflow in n8n. Here is the basic shape:

  1. Set up a trigger. This can be a schedule (run every morning at 8 am) or a webhook (triggered manually or by another system).
  1. Connect to your data source. If your invoices are in Xero, use the Xero node. If they are in a spreadsheet or database, use the appropriate connector. Pull unpaid invoices with their due dates.
  1. Filter the data. You only want to send reminders for invoices that are overdue by a specific number of days. Use a filter node to check: if today's date minus the due date equals 3, 10, or 20 days, include it.
  1. Build the email. Use a template node or the Gmail/SMTP node to compose the reminder. Include the invoice number, amount due, and original due date. Keep the tone professional but friendly.
  1. Send the email. Use your email provider's node (Gmail, Mailgun, SendGrid, or generic SMTP) to send it to the customer.
  1. Log the action. Add a step that records the reminder was sent. This can be a row in a spreadsheet, a database entry, or a note back in your accounting software.

Test the workflow with a test invoice before running it live. Check that the date calculation is correct and the email lands in your inbox.

Handling common problems

Duplicate reminders are the most common issue. If your workflow runs daily and checks for invoices that are exactly 3 days overdue, it might send the same reminder twice if there is a delay. Fix this by adding a flag in your data: once a reminder is sent for day 3, mark it so it does not send again.

Another issue is timezone confusion. If you run the workflow at 8 am NZ time but your customers are overseas, the date calculation might be off by a day. Use UTC timestamps in your workflow to avoid this.

Also check: does your accounting software allow you to mark an invoice as "reminder sent"? If not, you will need another way to track which invoices have been reminded. A simple spreadsheet column or a field in n8n's database connector works.

When automation is worth it (and when it is not)

Automate invoice reminders if:

  • You send invoices regularly (weekly or more often) and have a steady stream of unpaid ones.
  • Your customer list is stable enough that you are not constantly adding or removing people.
  • You have invoices with clear due dates and a consistent payment pattern.
  • Your team is spending time chasing the same invoices over and over.

Do not automate if:

  • You only invoice occasionally or have very few customers. Sending reminders by hand is faster than building the workflow.
  • Your invoices have complex payment terms or custom arrangements with each customer. Automation works best with standard rules.
  • Your customers need a personal touch or negotiation before they pay. A reminder email will not help if the real issue is a dispute or cash flow problem on their end.
  • Your accounting software does not easily export unpaid invoices. The data work to set it up will outweigh the benefit.

If you are on the fence, start small. Automate reminders for your most reliable customers first. Once you see it working and your team trusts it, expand to everyone.

Protecting your reputation

Automated reminders can feel impersonal if they are not done carefully. A few tips:

Personalise the email. Use the customer's name and reference their specific invoice. "Hi Sarah, Invoice #1234 for $500 is now 5 days overdue" feels better than a generic "Your payment is late."

Offer a way to respond. Include a contact email or phone number in case the customer has a question or dispute. Some late payments are not laziness; they are confusion or a genuine issue.

Do not spam. If a customer has already contacted you about a payment, skip the automated reminder that day. This requires manual override, which is fine for edge cases.

Review the reminders occasionally. Pull a sample of sent reminders and check they sound professional and are hitting the right customers. Adjust the tone or timing if needed.

Next steps

Start by mapping out your current process. How many invoices go unpaid each month? How many reminders do you send by hand? How long does it take?

Then pick one of your data sources (Xero, Wave, a spreadsheet) and test pulling the unpaid invoice list into n8n. This is the hardest part. Once the data flows, the rest is straightforward.

Build the workflow step by step. Test each part before connecting the next. Send yourself a test reminder and check it lands in your inbox and looks right.

Once it is live, monitor it for the first week. Check that reminders are going out on the right days and to the right people. Adjust the filter or email template if needed.

After a month, review the results. Are fewer invoices staying unpaid? Are customers paying faster? If yes, the automation is working. If not, the issue might not be reminders; it might be payment terms, customer selection, or something else.

Common questions

What if a customer has already paid but the payment has not cleared in my accounting software yet?

This is a timing issue. If you pull unpaid invoices in the morning but payments come through during the day, you might send a reminder for an invoice that is already paid. The fix is to add a buffer: only send reminders for invoices overdue by at least 2 days, not 1. This gives time for payments to sync.

Can I send different reminders based on how late the invoice is?

Yes. Use conditional logic in n8n to branch the workflow. If overdue by 3 days, send a friendly reminder. If overdue by 15 days, send a firmer notice. If overdue by 30 days, escalate to a phone call or a different email address. Each branch can have its own email template and recipient.
