How to automate cold email follow-ups in NZ

Cold email follow-ups are where most deals happen, but sending them by hand drains time and consistency. Automation lets you send the right message to the right person at the right time without touching your keyboard each time.

Nimish Rangani23 October 20267 min read

This guide covers how to set up follow-up sequences that work in New Zealand, what legal rules apply, and how to know whether automation is worth your effort.

Why follow-ups matter more than first emails

Most people don't reply to your first email. They're busy, distracted, or genuinely interested but forgot. A follow-up sequence gives you multiple chances to reach them without looking pushy.

Manual follow-ups fail because you forget who you've already contacted, when you contacted them, or what you said. You end up either spamming people with duplicate messages or letting warm leads go cold.

Automation solves this by tracking every contact, timing every follow-up, and sending consistent messages based on rules you set once.

Automation doesn't change these rules. You still need consent or a legitimate business reason to email someone. You still need a real unsubscribe link that works. Automation just makes it easier to comply consistently.

If someone unsubscribes, your automation must remove them from all future sends. This is non-negotiable and is where many setups fail.

The basic setup: email provider, workflow tool, and tracking

A working cold email automation has three parts:

  1. Email provider: Gmail, Outlook, or a dedicated tool like Mailgun or SendGrid. Check whether your provider allows bulk sending and what limits apply. Some free tiers restrict volume or flag bulk mail as spam.
  1. Workflow automation tool: Software like n8n, Zapier, or Make that connects your email provider to your contact list and timing rules. This is the engine that decides when to send what.
  1. Contact tracking: A database (spreadsheet, CRM, or SQL database) that records who you've emailed, when, what you sent, and whether they replied. Without this, you'll lose track and send duplicates.

You don't need all three to be fancy. A simple setup might be a spreadsheet, a Gmail account, and a tool that checks the spreadsheet every day and sends emails based on rules. A more robust setup uses a proper database and a dedicated workflow platform.

The key is that your system must know: has this person already been contacted? Did they reply? Should they get the next follow-up?

When automation is worth doing (and when it is not)

Automation makes sense if you're sending cold emails regularly and the list is large enough that manual tracking becomes a burden. If you only send a handful of emails a week and follow up by hand, the time to set up automation probably isn't worth it.

Automation also makes sense if you want consistency. If you're worried that some prospects get three follow-ups and others get one, or that you forget to follow up at all, automation removes that guesswork.

Do not automate if:

  • Your contact list is tiny (fewer than a few dozen people). Manual follow-up is faster.
  • You don't have a clear, compliant reason to email these people. Automation won't fix a bad list.
  • Your email domain or sending IP has a poor reputation. Automation will amplify spam complaints and damage your deliverability further. Fix your reputation first.
  • You're not sure whether your recipients have opted in or given permission. Automation makes compliance violations scale faster.
  • You plan to send the same generic message to everyone. Personalisation matters for cold email, and automation without personalisation tends to get ignored or reported as spam.

If you're unsure, start small. Send a few manual follow-ups first, measure the reply rate, and then decide whether automation is worth the setup cost.

A simple workflow: timing and sequence logic

Here's how a basic follow-up sequence works:

  1. You upload a list of contacts to your database (with email, name, company, or whatever you have).
  2. Your workflow tool sends the first email immediately or on a schedule you set.
  3. It marks that contact as 'first email sent' and records the date and time.
  4. After a set number of days (say, three days), the tool checks: did they reply?
  5. If no reply, it sends the second email and marks the contact as 'follow-up 1 sent'.
  6. After another interval, it sends a third email.
  7. After the final email, it marks the contact as 'sequence complete' and stops.

You can add logic to stop the sequence early if someone replies or unsubscribes. You can also add branches: if they opened the email but didn't reply, send a different follow-up than if they didn't open it at all.

The intervals and number of follow-ups depend on your industry and what works for you. A common pattern is three emails over two weeks, but this varies. Test different sequences and measure reply rates to find what works.

Make sure your tool logs everything. You need to see which emails were sent, when, to whom, and what the outcome was. This data helps you improve your sequence over time.

Avoiding spam filters and deliverability problems

Automated emails are more likely to be flagged as spam because they're sent in bulk. Here's how to protect your sender reputation:

  • Use a consistent, recognisable sender name and email address. Don't rotate addresses or names.
  • Set up SPF, DKIM, and DMARC records for your domain. These tell email providers that your emails are legitimate. Your email provider or domain registrar can guide you through this.
  • Warm up your sending IP if you're using a dedicated one. Start with small volumes and gradually increase. Don't send thousands of emails on day one.
  • Include a real unsubscribe link in every email. Don't make it hard to find. Honour unsubscribe requests immediately.
  • Monitor bounce rates and spam complaints. If too many emails bounce or get reported as spam, pause and investigate.
  • Avoid spam trigger words ("free", "act now", "limited time"). These are old advice but still matter.
  • Test your emails in a spam checker before you automate them. Services like MXToolbox or similar tools can flag obvious problems.

If your emails start landing in spam folders, the problem is usually sender reputation, not the automation tool. Check your domain's reputation and fix any authentication issues first.

Tools to consider

There's no single right tool. The choice depends on your budget, technical comfort, and how much customisation you need.

Email providers: Gmail (free but limited for bulk), Outlook, Mailgun, SendGrid, or a dedicated cold email tool like Lemlist or Instantly. Check what each allows for automation and what volume limits apply.

Workflow tools: n8n, Zapier, Make, or Integromat. These connect your email provider to your contact list and handle the logic. Some are easier for non-technical users; others are more flexible.

CRM or database: Airtable, Notion, or a proper CRM like Pipedrive. These store your contacts and track replies. A spreadsheet works for small lists but gets messy fast.

Spam checkers: MXToolbox, Mail-tester, or similar. Use these to test your emails before you send them at scale.

Start with tools you already use or that have a free tier. You can upgrade later if you need more power.

Common questions

Do I need permission to send cold emails in New Zealand?

In New Zealand, laws such as the Unsolicited Electronic Messages Act 2007 and the Privacy Act 2020 can apply here. This is general information, not legal advice, so check the current requirements before you send.

What happens if someone unsubscribes?

Your automation must remove them from all future sends immediately. This means updating your database so they never receive another email from your sequence. If you don't do this, you're breaking the law and damaging your sender reputation.

How do I know if my automation is working?

Track open rates, reply rates, and bounce rates. Check your database to confirm that emails are being sent on schedule, that replies are being recorded, and that unsubscribes are being honoured. If reply rates are very low or bounce rates are high, investigate your sender reputation and email content before blaming the tool.
